How this was worked out

Zones. There is no public map file of Uganda's official agro-ecological zones, so the zones were built from the FEWS NET Uganda livelihood zones (2013), which cover the whole country and are drawn largely on agro-ecology. Their 42 zones were grouped into the national ten-zone scheme, with the Mt Elgon highlands kept separate from the eastern lowlands. Lakeshore fishing, urban and national park areas were left out.

Rainfall. CHIRPS daily rainfall (about 5 km grid, satellite and rain-gauge records, 1981 to today) was averaged over each zone by SERVIR's ClimateSERV service. Only complete years, 1981 to 2025, are used.

Start of the rains. The first day, inside the usual planting window, with at least 20 mm over three days, two of them rainy, and no run of seven dry days in the following 30 days. That last check means a false start does not count as the start.

End of the rains. A simple soil water balance is filled by rain from the start of the season and loses 4 mm a day to evaporation, holding at most 70 mm. The season ends on the first day, inside the usual window for the end, on which that store runs dry.

Seasons. West Nile, Mid-Northern, Karamoja, the Eastern lowlands and Mt Elgon are treated as having one long main season (start from mid February to mid June). The other zones have a first season (start from February to April) and a second season (start from August to October).

Trends and caveats. Trends use the Theil-Sen slope and the Mann-Kendall test at the 5% level. Because the rain is averaged over a whole zone, a single farm sees more completely dry days and longer dry spells than these figures show, so the changes over time matter more than the exact values. Temperature is ERA5 reanalysis at one central point per zone.
